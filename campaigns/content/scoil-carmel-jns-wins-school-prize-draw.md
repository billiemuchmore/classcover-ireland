Scoil Carmel JNS in Firhouse, Dublin 24 has won the €500 school prize draw that ran alongside our school infopack in the INTO newsletter. The entry came from Maria Jones, the school's deputy principal. Then something better happened. [ABC School Supplies](https://abcschoolsupplies.ie/) matched the prize, so the school now has €1,000 going into literacy resources.

## A €500 voucher that became €1,000

The draw was a simple thing. Any primary or post-primary school in the Republic of Ireland could request the ClassCover school infopack and go into the hat for a €500 One4All voucher for their school. Free to enter, one winner, drawn at random.

Scoil Carmel came out. By the time we were talking to Maria Jones about it, she had already been on to ABC School Supplies, and they had agreed to match the €500 with a voucher of their own.

> "We are thrilled with our voucher. ABC School Supplies have agreed to match it, so we will be investing in some literacy resources."
>
> — Maria Jones, Deputy Principal, Scoil Carmel JNS

Nobody asked ABC to do that. They are an Irish supplier who work with schools every day, and they chose to double a prize for a school that had won it fairly. That is worth saying out loud.

## Where the €1,000 is going

Scoil Carmel JNS is a junior school. It takes children from Junior Infants to Second Class, so every child in the building is somewhere in the first few years of learning to read. [The school moved to its grounds on the Firhouse Road in 1954](https://www.scoilcarmeljns.com/ourschool) and became a junior school in 1977, when the parish filled up with young families.

The money is going into new reading books and other literacy resources. For a school with that enrolment, it is exactly the right shelf to be filling.

## Why we ran a draw in the first place

Irish schools are dealing with a substitute problem that is thoroughly documented and getting no easier. In the INTO teacher shortage survey published in November 2025, [60% of the 565 primary and special schools that responded said they had been unable to source a substitute for an absence](https://www.into.ie/2025/11/03/teacher-shortage-survey-special-schools-deis-and-irish-medium-education-continue-to-be-worst-hit/). The same survey found 215 schools splitting classes to get through it, costing 735 days of disrupted learning in the first six weeks of the school year alone. We have pulled the full picture together in [Ireland's substitute teacher shortage](/blog/substitute-teacher-shortage-ireland/).

The infopack sets out what ClassCover is and how a school starts using it. Schools book the subs they already trust, instead of posting a job and waiting to see who answers. That distinction is the whole argument, and we make it properly in [why Ireland needs a booking platform, not another jobs board](/blog/substitute-teacher-booking-platform-vs-jobs-board-ireland/).

The draw was there because getting a principal or a deputy principal to stop and read anything in September takes more than a good subject line. That part worked. The winner turning the prize into twice the prize was not in the plan.
